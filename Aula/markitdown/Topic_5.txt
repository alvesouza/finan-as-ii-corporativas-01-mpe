Threat of termination

       Lenders have to incentivize repayment by engaging in some wasteful
       (inefﬁcient) activity

              In this model, the inefﬁciency comes from the inefﬁcient liquidation, as
              E(R˜2) > L

       Costly collateral pledging interpretation: we can interpret L as the
       value that the lender obtains by seizing some collateral (an asset).
       Inefﬁciency comes from the fact that this asset is worth more when it
      is in the borrower’s hands: E(R˜2) > L

       Room for renegotiation: after the termination decision, but before its
       implementation, the borrower can offer a bribe slight above L (and
      smaller than E(R˜2) to the lender, who would be happy to accept. This
       prospect ex ante undermines incentives to pay out income, and
       reduces welfare

The recursive equilibrium for this economy is deﬁned by a set of policy
functions, deﬁned over the state variables s = (B, y), for (i) consumption
c(s); (ii) government’s assets holdings B (s) and default sets D(B); and
