Discuss at graduate depth (1/3) six Annual Review of Financial Economics surveys, screened for a listener with two interests: green finance and quantitative trading. Episode two goes deep on the green pick; episode three pits the quant candidates against each other and gives the final verdict. Sources: `Artigo_Corporate Social Responsibility.pdf` (Hong and Shore 2023), `Artigo_The Marginal Value of Cash.pdf` (Bolton, Chen and Wang 2024), `Artigo_The Benefits and Costs of Secured Debt.pdf` (Benmelech 2024), `Artigo_Two Tales of Debt.pdf` (Kermani and Ma 2026), `Artigo_Debt Structure.pdf` (Colla, Ippolito and Li 2020), `Artigo_Private or Public Equity.pdf` (Ewens and Farre-Mensa 2022). Listeners know Modigliani–Miller, the trade-off theory, asymmetric information and pledgeable income from the course slides (`Aula_Topic2A - Valuation and MM.pdf` through `Aula_Topic5 - Nonverifiable income and sovereign debt.pdf`). No arithmetic on air; quote numbers as the articles report them.

Scoring rules. Score every article on both lenses, out loud, with reasons. Green lens: does it model or measure environmental externalities, green pricing or transition investment, or does it only offer machinery a green-finance analyst could borrow? When an article has no green content, say so plainly and never invent one; any green application the hosts propose must be labelled as the listener's extension, not the authors' claim. Quant lens: does it give a priced, measurable relation (a return or spread on a variable a researcher could sort on in CRSP, Compustat, DealScan or ISS), a credible identification design, and warnings about the traps a backtest would hit (endogeneity, selection, omitted risk factors, short samples)? For each article, also give the sample period, say whether the evidence predates 2020, and say whether a master's student could rebuild the data.

Hong and Shore: pecuniary versus nonpecuniary motives for CSR; the equilibrium wedge r_S minus r_U equals minus m over q (Hong, Wang and Yang 2023); seven tests; 80.3% of 56 papers back the nonpecuniary view; sin stocks beat comparables by about 3% a year; the green-minus-brown wedge of Pástor, Stambaugh and Taylor; their own regression discontinuity on ISS votes, 2006–2021; a mean greenium of −1.41% on the WACC, and why the decarbonisation investment it implies overshoots actual spending.

Bolton, Chen and Wang: the marginal value of cash as the wedge between internal and external funds; marginal q equals physical cost times the marginal value of cash; a conditional beta proportional to p'(w)/p(w), and why the textbook unlevering formula misstates it by up to a quarter or a third; idiosyncratic volatility moving priced beta; Palazzo's 0.69% monthly spread between top and bottom cash deciles; cash-rich firms outperforming in late 2007 and early 2020.

Benmelech: selection makes secured loans look 150–200 basis points dearer; the firm-by-time fixed-effect secured premium of Benmelech, Kumar and Rajan, about 2 basis points for investment grade and 55 below it, rising steeply from BBB to CCC; the contingent use of security; secured bonds falling from 98.5% of issuance in 1900 to 7.5% in 2000.

Kermani and Ma: asset-based debt (about 20%) versus cash flow–based debt (about 80%); total borrowing independent of physical assets for large or profitable firms but tied to them for small or loss-making ones; within-firm interest-rate comparisons across debt types; earnings covenants, the interest-coverage channel of monetary policy, weaker asset-price amplification.

Colla, Ippolito and Li: maturity and priority; bank loans, bonds, credit lines, commercial paper; more than three quarters of US firms concentrate their borrowing in one debt type (HHI and Excl90); bankruptcy costs, monitoring and market access as explanations.

Ewens and Farre-Mensa: founders retain 30% more equity; late-stage private capital up from 14.2 to 80 billion dollars between 2002 and 2019; fewer and older IPOs; NSMIA, SOX and the JOBS Act; mutual and hedge funds moving into private rounds.

Verdict. Rank the six on each lens and defend each ranking against its strongest objection. Test this provisional ranking rather than assume it: Hong and Shore is the only article with direct green content; on the quant lens, Bolton, Chen and Wang lead, Benmelech is the credit-market challenger, and Hong and Shore compete through their return estimates and event-study design. Close by naming what episode two (Hong and Shore in full) and episode three (Bolton, Chen and Wang against Benmelech, then the overall verdict) will examine.
