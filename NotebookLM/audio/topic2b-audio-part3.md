Explain and discuss at graduate depth (3/3) Topic 2B of Corporate Finance, taxes, financial distress and the static trade-off — exercises and misconceptions. Listeners heard episode one (the frictions and the evidence) and episode two (the tax-shield formulas, WACC versus APV, Miller, risk-shifting, debt overhang, the trade-off condition). Sources: `Aula_Topic2B - Taxes, distress and trade-off.pdf` (the slides), `Lista_PS2.pdf`, `Lista_R3 - MM, taxes and trade-off.md` (hereafter Lista_R3), `Livro_Berk DeMarzo Harford - Fundamentals of Corporate Finance 2e (2011).pdf` (hereafter Berk) and `Livro_Grinblatt Titman - Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy 2e (2002).pdf` (hereafter Grinblatt–Titman). No arithmetic on air: for each exercise, say what it asks, the logic of the solution in words, and the trap it sets.

The tax shield. Lista_R3 exercise 3 (Ross 16.14): with perpetual safe debt the levered firm is worth the unlevered firm plus the tax rate times the debt. Exercise 4 (Higgins 8.23): the APV as the base-case NPV plus the present value of the interest tax shields on constant debt. Exercise 6 (Berk 16.11): shields on amortising debt, each discounted at the debt rate. Exercise 9 (Grinblatt–Titman 13.15–13.16): the cost of equity and the after-tax WACC with riskless perpetual debt, then after a debt-for-equity swap (Hamada). Exercise 13 (Ross 16.19): derive the MM adjusted cost of capital. Exercise 12 (Berk 16.18): a leveraged recapitalisation with taxes; why the share price jumps at the announcement and not at the repurchase, and how many shares are bought back at that price.

PS2 Exercise 2: one firm under three debt policies: a constant debt-to-equity ratio, interest set as a share of free cash flow, and a fixed amount of perpetual debt. Say which discount rate fits the shield in each case and why the three values differ.

Personal taxes. Lista_R3 exercise 14 (Amaro de Matos 2.2.7 #1): prove Miller's gain from leverage with corporate and personal taxes, and say when it is zero.

Distress. PS2 Exercise 3: a two-outcome project financed with debt, first with no frictions, then with lawyers taking a share of the cash flow in bankruptcy; in part (c), explain why creditors demand a higher face value, so the shareholders bear the expected cost up front. Lista_R3 exercise 8 (Berk 16.22): back out the present value of financial distress costs from the share price. Exercise 10 (Grinblatt–Titman 16.2): the static trade-off in two states, the tax saving per dollar of debt against a fixed bankruptcy cost, and how this corner solution differs from the smooth first-order condition.

Payout. Lista_R3 exercise 2 (Berk 17.7): a dividend versus a repurchase in a perfect market, the ex-dividend price against the price after a buyback, and why shareholder wealth is the same. PS2 Exercise 1(f): the entrepreneur borrows more to pay a dividend today; the firm's value falls by the dividend and the entrepreneur is no better off.

Name and correct three misconceptions, each with its mechanism: the tax shield makes debt free (it is worth less once personal taxes, risk and distress costs are counted); bankruptcy itself destroys value (the transfer of control does not, deadweight costs do); the optimal capital structure maximises the expected return on equity (it maximises firm value; a higher expected return on equity is compensation for risk).
