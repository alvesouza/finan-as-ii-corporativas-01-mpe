Explain and discuss at graduate depth (3/3) Topic 3 of Corporate Finance, asymmetric information — exercises and misconceptions. Listeners heard episode one (concepts and evidence) and episode two (pooling and breakdown, the bureau, the pecking order, Stiglitz–Weiss, collateral signalling, Leland–Pyle, Rock). Sources: `Aula_Topic3 - Asymmetric information.pdf` (the slides), `Lista_R7 - Lectures 3-5.md` (hereafter Lista_R7), `Lista_R5 - Asymmetric information.md` (hereafter Lista_R5), `Livro_Jean Tirole - The Theory of Corporate Finance-Princeton University Press (2005).pdf` (hereafter Tirole) and `Livro_de Matos, João Amaro - Theoretical Foundations of Corporate Finance (2002, Princeton University Press) [10.1515_9780691188478] - libgen.li.pdf` (hereafter Amaro de Matos). No arithmetic on air: for each exercise, say what it asks, the logic of the solution in words, and the trap it sets.

IPO underpricing. Lista_R7 exercise 1 (Ross 15.3): two IPOs at the same price, one underpriced and one overpriced, and you are rationed only on the good one; explain why the gain you expect is far below the gain you would get with full allocations, and name the principle. Exercise 6 (Amaro de Matos 6.2.7 #4 and #6): why an uninformed investor is less likely to be allocated an underpriced issue than an overpriced one, and why the offer price must fall as the informed share rises.

Pecking order. Lista_R7 exercise 5 (Grinblatt–Titman 19.6): management values the firm above the market; count the shares needed to fund a positive-NPV project, then compare the share price once the truth comes out with and without the issue. Explain why a good project can be passed up. Link to Lista_R5 exercise 7 (Amaro de Matos 3.2.6 #4, Myers–Majluf) and exercise 3 (Grinblatt–Titman 19.4).

Pooling and breakdown. Lista_R5 exercise 6 (Tirole 6.1): two types differing in private benefit; why the lender cannot fund only good borrowers, the threshold share of good types, and the cross-subsidy. Lista_R7 exercise 11 (Tirole 6.2) extends it to a continuum of types and a monopoly lender: the share of borrowers who behave rises with the borrower's stake, low-quality types force a rate that earns profits on high-quality ones, and with a uniform distribution the market can break down.

Information and signals. Lista_R7 exercise 19 (Tirole 6.7, Padilla–Pagano): banks sharing default data lose local market power but protect borrowers' investment; explain why they refuse to share when talent is fixed and choose to share when the borrower's quality is endogenous. Lista_R5 exercise 9 (Tirole 6.6): buying a costly rating to separate; exercise 10 (Tirole 6.10): short maturity as a costless signal. Lista_R7 exercise 20 (Amaro de Matos 3.2.6 #2): Leland–Pyle with mean-variance utility, debt versus the retained ownership share.

Name and correct four misconceptions, each with its mechanism: a lender facing excess demand can always raise the rate until the market clears (Stiglitz–Weiss: the pool worsens); more information always raises total value (the bureau may only redistribute); underpricing hands uninformed investors a free gain (they only break even, after the winner's curse); good firms prefer debt because it is cheaper (debt is preferred because it is less sensitive to what insiders know, and bad types copy them).

Close by connecting forward: Topic 4A turns from hidden information to hidden action, where debt is again optimal, now because it gives the strongest incentives.
