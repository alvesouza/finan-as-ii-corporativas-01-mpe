Explain and discuss at graduate depth Topic 2B of Corporate Finance: corporate taxes, financial distress and the static trade-off, as the first departures from Modigliani–Miller. Sources: `Aula_Topic2B - Taxes, distress and trade-off.pdf` (the slides), `Lista_PS2.pdf`, `Lista_R3 - MM, taxes and trade-off.md`, `Livro_Berk DeMarzo Harford - Fundamentals of Corporate Finance 2e (2011).pdf` (hereafter Berk, Ch. 16–17), `Livro_Grinblatt Titman - Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy 2e (2002).pdf` (hereafter Grinblatt–Titman, Ch. 13–16) and `Livro_de Matos, João Amaro - Theoretical Foundations of Corporate Finance (2002, Princeton University Press) [10.1515_9780691188478] - libgen.li.pdf` (hereafter Amaro de Matos, Ch. 2). No arithmetic on air. Derive each result in words, state its assumptions, and say where it breaks. Listeners know MM I, MM II and unlevering from Topic 2A.

The tax shield: the friction is the different tax treatment of interest and of equity payouts. Argue why the levered firm can pay investors more each period, why all of the gain goes to shareholders, and why homemade leverage cannot copy it. Use Lista_R3 exercise 12 (Berk 16.18, a leveraged recap: when the price moves and how many shares are repurchased). Edge case from the slide appendix: with risky debt the shield follows the coupon, not the expected return on debt.

How risky is the shield: fixed debt makes it as safe as the debt; a target debt-equity ratio makes it as risky as the assets. Explain the three closed forms with growth, including why annual rebalancing leaves the next shield known one period ahead. Link the slides' constant-debt WACC to the MM adjusted cost of capital in Lista_R3 exercise 13 (Ross 16.19), and walk through exercises 6 (amortising debt) and 9 (Grinblatt–Titman 13.15–13.16, Hamada) verbally.

Lista_R3 exercises 3 and 4 give the baseline: with perpetual safe debt the shield is worth the tax rate times the debt, and the APV is the base-case NPV plus that value.

WACC versus APV: argue why the two coincide when the debt-equity ratio is constant, retracing the appendix recursion that turns a cash flow plus a tax shield discounted at the levered return into free cash flow discounted at the after-tax WACC. Say exactly when one WACC fails and why APV absorbs any known debt schedule, plus distress costs. Discuss PS2 Exercise 2 and its three debt policies.

Personal taxes: extend the slides with Miller (1977). Explain why the corporate advantage can vanish in equilibrium once interest is taxed at the investor level, and DeAngelo–Masulis on non-debt tax shields (Amaro de Matos §2.2, Lista_R3 exercise 14, Grinblatt–Titman Ch. 14).

Distress: transfer of control is not an MM violation; deadweight costs are. Separate direct from indirect costs and judge the evidence: car prices and default risk (Hortaçsu et al., 2013); job applicants and talent (Brown and Matsa, 2016; Baghai et al., 2021); unemployment insurance as a subsidy to risky firms (Van Doornik et al., 2022). Then the agency costs. Using Topic 2A's equity-as-a-call, explain why risk-shifting follows from convexity and debt overhang from the senior claim taking part of any new project's value (Grinblatt–Titman Ch. 16; the bridge to Tirole Ch. 3). Ask who bears expected distress costs ex ante (PS2 Exercise 3(c)), and how a share price implies their present value (Lista_R3 exercise 8, Berk 16.22).

The trade-off model: derive the first-order condition in words. A marginal unit of face value earns the tax rate times the probability of solvency and costs the bankruptcy cost times the probability density at the default threshold. Sign the effects of the tax rate and the cost, contrast them with the two-state version (Lista_R3 exercise 10), and explain why the 100%-debt prediction fails and what Jagannathan et al. (2017) and Graham et al. (2015) show.

Bank capital: judge Admati et al.'s three claims, that more equity lowers ROE, that equity is expensive, and that it loses the tax shield. Answer the first two with MM II. Separate the private cost in the third (the lost shield, a transfer from taxpayers) from the social benefit of fewer failures.

Payout: derive dividend irrelevance from sources equal to uses, then break it: dividend taxes (Berk §17.3, Grinblatt–Titman Ch. 15) and signalling (Berk §17.5), which opens asymmetric information.

Correct three misconceptions: the tax shield makes debt free; bankruptcy itself destroys value; the optimal structure maximises the expected return on equity. Close with PS2 Exercise 1(f): a debt-financed dividend leaves the entrepreneur no better off.
