Explain and describe in detail (2/2) Class 1 of Corporate Finance: the Modigliani–Miller benchmark and the one assumption whose failure explains the stylized facts. Using the uploaded source `Livro_Jean Tirole - The Theory of Corporate Finance-Princeton University Press (2005).pdf` (hereafter Tirole; printed pages), Ch. 2 §2.2 (pp. 77–79) and §3.2.1–3.2.2 (pp. 115–118), this episode proves MM, confronts it with the facts from Part 1, and derives credit rationing. Graduate level: say every derivation step aloud, state each assumption and show where it is used. Open with the puzzle: a founder has a project costing 100 thousand reais with expected return 128, only 10 of her own, and every investor refuses.

MM claim (pp. 77–78): total firm value is independent of financial structure; the pie's size does not depend on how it is carved. Value additivity: with debt face value D, equity pays max(0, R − D) and debt min(R, D). Do the case split aloud: if R ≥ D the sum is (R − D) + D = R; if R < D it is 0 + R = R. Take expectations: V_E + V_D = E(R), independent of D. Use the example R = 0 or 200 with equal probability: D = 80 gives debt 40, equity 60; D = 150 gives 75 and 25; the total is 100 both times. Explain footnote 9: risk neutrality is not needed, because state-contingent prices give the same additivity. Mention that `Livro_de Matos, João Amaro - Theoretical Foundations of Corporate Finance (2002, Princeton University Press) [10.1515_9780691188478] - libgen.li.pdf` §2.1 proves MM1 by arbitrage, Prop. 2.1.1 (p. 42).

Payout irrelevance (p. 78): state the arbitrage condition P_t = βE[d_{t+1} + P_{t+1}] and the cash identity R_t + P_t(n_t − n_{t−1}) = n_{t−1}d_t + I_t. Walk through substituting the identity at t+1 into V_t = n_tP_t to get V_t = βE[R_{t+1} − I_{t+1} + V_{t+1}], then iterate to the discounted sum of R − I. Explain why dividends and issues drop out.

Where it breaks (p. 79): when managerial decisions cannot be specified contractually, "the split of the pie matters". Run the founder through MM: positive NPV should mean funded. Recall Part 1's facts (retentions, little equity, negative announcement returns) as evidence that MM is missing something.

The fixed-investment model (§3.2.1, pp. 115–116): investment I, net worth A < I, success R or failure 0, behave with probability p_H and no private benefit, shirk with p_L and private benefit B, Δp = p_H − p_L, risk neutrality, zero rate, limited liability, competitive lenders. Assumptions (3.1) p_HR − I > 0 and (3.2) p_LR − I + B < 0; explain Tirole's decomposition showing (3.2) rules out any loan that tolerates shirking. Calibration: I = 100, A = 10, R = 160, p_H = 0.8, p_L = 0.4, B = 20; check NPV 28 and shirking value −16.

Credit analysis (§3.2.2, pp. 116–117): derive the incentive constraint ΔpR_b ≥ B, so the minimum stake is B/Δp = 50; the most that can be pledged on success is R − B/Δp = 110; pledgeable income P = p_H(R − B/Δp) = 88. Lenders' breakeven needs P ≥ I − A = 90, which rearranges to A ≥ Ā = p_HB/Δp − (p_HR − I), equation (3.3): agency rent 40 minus NPV 28 gives 12. She has 10, so she is rationed by 2, and show that P − (I − A) = A − Ā = −2. Explain condition (3.4), why a rationed borrower's offer of a higher rate is refused, "one only lends to the rich", the gross rate 1 + ι = 1/p_H as a pure default premium, and the jump in utility (3.6) from 0 to the whole NPV at Ā.

Comparative statics (p. 118): Ā rises with B (∂Ā/∂B = p_H/Δp = 2), with p_L and with I, and falls with R and p_H; interpret the likelihood ratio Δp/p_H as the informativeness of output about effort. Work two cases aloud. A richer founder with A = 20: lenders need p_HR_l = 80, so R_l = 100 and her stake R_b = 60 exceeds 50, she behaves, and her net utility is exactly the NPV of 28. Our founder with B lowered to 15: the minimum stake falls to 37.5, pledgeable income rises to 98, Ā falls to 2, and she is funded; the largest B at which A = 10 suffices solves 2B − 28 = 10, so B = 19. Stress that better investor protection works by lowering B. Recover MM: set B = 0 and Ā = −28, so every positive-NPV project is funded. One assumption separates the two worlds.

Close by linking back to the facts: net worth explains reliance on retentions, collateral and steady cash flow raise pledgeable income, and monitoring lowers B. Misconceptions to correct: positive NPV does not imply funding; agency rent (expected) differs from the minimum stake (paid on success); the interest rate is a default premium, not investors' return.
