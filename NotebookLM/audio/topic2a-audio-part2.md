Explain and discuss at graduate depth (2/3) Topic 2A of Corporate Finance, valuation and the Modigliani–Miller benchmark — the derivations. Listeners heard episode one (discount rates, incremental cash flows, the NPV rule, debt and equity as contracts, complete markets as an idea); episode three works through the exercises. Sources: `Aula_Topic2A - Valuation and MM.pdf` (the slides), `Livro_Berk DeMarzo Harford - Fundamentals of Corporate Finance 2e (2011).pdf` (hereafter Berk), `Livro_Grinblatt Titman - Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy 2e (2002).pdf` (hereafter Grinblatt–Titman, Ch. 7, 11, 14), `Livro_de Matos, João Amaro - Theoretical Foundations of Corporate Finance (2002, Princeton University Press) [10.1515_9780691188478] - libgen.li.pdf` (hereafter Amaro de Matos, Ch. 1–2) and `Livro_Jean Tirole - The Theory of Corporate Finance-Princeton University Press (2005).pdf` (hereafter Tirole). No arithmetic on air. Derive each result in words, state exactly which assumptions it uses, and say what breaks when one fails.

Unlevering: assets equal debt plus equity in market value, and covariance is linear, so the asset beta is the value-weighted average of the debt beta and the equity beta. Derive it, then the edge cases: risky debt has a nonzero beta, so take it from bond indices rather than set it to zero; averaging comparables' equity betas mixes their different leverage, so unlever each first, average, then relever at the target; with taxes the tax shield enters the market-value balance sheet and changes the formula (Topic 2B).

Complete markets: show how replication and the law of one price price any claim, and argue why the absence of arbitrage is equivalent to the existence of strictly positive state prices (Amaro de Matos §1.2, Grinblatt–Titman Ch. 7). Derive Fisher separation under uncertainty: with complete markets, every owner agrees to maximise the market value of the firm's investment, whatever their preferences and endowments, because they can trade to any consumption pattern afterwards. Ask what changes when markets are incomplete and a new security can span a new state.

The penniless entrepreneur, a proof by construction: price the project from a traded stock and bond, then show that under debt, equity or any mix the firm is worth the same, and the entrepreneur keeps the whole NPV because every financier breaks even. Note the slides' check: replicating each position directly with traded securities gives the same answer.

MM I, two proofs. First, arbitrage with homemade leverage: an investor can copy any capital structure on personal account, so two firms with the same assets cannot trade at different total values; say why this needs investors to borrow on the same terms as firms. Second, with risky debt: risky debt is a safe bond minus a put on the assets, equity is a call, and put–call parity makes their sum equal the assets whatever the face value (Berk §21.5–21.6). Then Tirole's state-by-state identity (Ch. 2 §2.2, p. 78): in every state the payments to all claims add up to the firm's income, so total value cannot depend on the split; explain why this needs no risk neutrality, and its one real failure: the claim structure changes the entrepreneur's non-contractible effort, and so the income itself.

MM II: derive that the expected return on equity rises linearly in the debt-to-equity ratio while debt is safe, then less than linearly as debt becomes risky and its expected return rises, while the WACC, the return on assets, stays fixed. State the conservation-of-value principle. Explain why the promised yield overstates the expected cost of risky debt: the yield is earned only if the firm does not default.

Close by stating what episode three will cover: the PS1, PS2 and list exercises that apply these results, and four misconceptions.
