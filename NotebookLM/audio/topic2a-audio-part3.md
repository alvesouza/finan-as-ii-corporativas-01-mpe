Explain and discuss at graduate depth (3/3) Topic 2A of Corporate Finance, valuation and the Modigliani–Miller benchmark — exercises and misconceptions. Listeners heard episode one (discount rates, cash flows, the NPV rule, debt and equity) and episode two (unlevering, complete markets, Fisher separation, MM I and II). Sources: `Aula_Topic2A - Valuation and MM.pdf` (the slides), `Lista_PS1.pdf`, `Lista_PS2.pdf`, `Lista_R2 - Valuation.md` (hereafter Lista_R2), `Lista_R3 - MM, taxes and trade-off.md` (hereafter Lista_R3), `Livro_Berk DeMarzo Harford - Fundamentals of Corporate Finance 2e (2011).pdf` (hereafter Berk) and `Livro_Grinblatt Titman - Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy 2e (2002).pdf` (hereafter Grinblatt–Titman). No arithmetic on air: for each exercise, say what it asks, the logic of the solution in words, and the trap it sets.

Cash flows and the NPV rule. PS1 Exercise 2: a consultant's earnings forecast turned into free cash flows; why the consulting fee is sunk, why only the overhead that the project causes counts, how the tax rate is read off the report, and how working capital invested at the start and recovered at the end enters. PS1 Exercise 3: replacing a one-year-old machine; why the old machine's purchase price is sunk but its book value still matters, because selling below book value creates a tax-deductible loss, and how the decision can flip if that tax saving is ignored. Lista_R2 exercise 11 (Berk 9.25): from earnings to free cash flow with working capital and allocated overhead. Lista_R2 exercise 10 (Higgins 8.16): enterprise versus equity IRR, and why each must be compared with its own hurdle rate.

Discount rates. Lista_R2 exercise 6 (Grinblatt–Titman 11.10–11.13): unlever the comparables Lockheed and Northrop, average the asset betas, relever, and value the Hughes acquisition as a growing perpetuity. Lista_R2 exercise 9 (Berk 13.20): a divisional WACC from a pure-play comparable. IRR pitfalls: Lista_R2 exercise 7 (Berk 8.26, ranking mutually exclusive projects and the crossover rate) and exercise 8 (Berk 8.17, multiple IRRs from a perpetual outflow).

Complete markets. Lista_R2 exercise 14: recover state prices from traded assets and value a project with them. Exercise 15: the proof of Fisher separation under uncertainty, and which step uses completeness.

MM. PS2 Exercise 1 (a)–(e): state prices from a stock and a bond, calls and puts on the project, put–call parity across two strikes and what it says about the face value of debt, then the returns on debt, equity and assets for every debt–equity mix, with the return on assets unchanged. Lista_R3 exercise 15 (Amaro de Matos 2.1.5 #3–4): MM I with perpetual debt, and how it fails when investors borrow at a higher rate than firms. Lista_R3 exercise 11 (Grinblatt–Titman 14.2–14.3): MM surviving risky debt, and new debt of equal seniority that dilutes the old creditors and transfers value to shareholders. Lista_R3 exercise 5 (Berk 16.5): why maximising the expected return on equity is not the goal. PS2 Exercise 4 (Yerba): earnings per share rise, the P/E falls, and no value is created. Retell the slides' "borrow more and the value rises" fallacy and find the error in it.

Name and correct four misconceptions, each with its mechanism: debt is cheap financing (its low rate is offset by a higher cost of equity, MM II); MM needs risk neutrality (the state-by-state identity does not); a positive NPV always helps shareholders (debt overhang and asymmetric information); the equity beta measures project risk (it mixes asset risk with leverage).
