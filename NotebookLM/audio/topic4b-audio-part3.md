Explain and discuss at graduate depth (3/3) Topic 4B of Corporate Finance, asset substitution, debt overhang and debt renegotiation — exercises and misconceptions. Listeners heard episode one (concepts and evidence) and episode two (the example's prices, the overhang wedge, renegotiation, default as insurance, Karlan–Zinman). Sources: `Aula_Topic4B - Asset substitution, debt overhang and renegotiation.pdf` (the slides), `Lista_R7 - Lectures 3-5.md` (hereafter Lista_R7), `Lista_R4 - Moral hazard and financing capacity.md` (hereafter Lista_R4), `Livro_Jean Tirole - The Theory of Corporate Finance-Princeton University Press (2005).pdf` (hereafter Tirole) and `Livro_Grinblatt Titman - Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy 2e (2002).pdf` (hereafter Grinblatt–Titman). No arithmetic on air: for each exercise, say what it asks, the logic of the solution in words, and the trap it sets.

Asset substitution. Lista_R7 exercise 4 (Grinblatt–Titman 16.1): a firm whose cash just matches its debt picks between a risky and a nearly safe project; compare the NPVs, then explain why shareholders want the risky one. Exercise 7 (Berk 16.26, Zymase): three research strategies; how the strategy shareholders prefer changes as the debt due rises, and the agency cost as the value lost against the best strategy. Lista_R4 exercise 3 (Berk 16.24) adds a gas-pipeline firm tempted by risk.

Debt overhang. Lista_R7 exercise 8 (Grinblatt–Titman 16.12–16.13, Ajax): the NPV of a project and whether it can be funded when the existing senior bond bars new debt; then a capital-intensive version whose cash flow in a recession is wiped out by the debt, and whether new equity can fund it. Exercise 9 (Grinblatt–Titman 16.3): liquidate now, or take a safe or a risky project, with a senior bond and a subordinated loan; whether the junior lenders should lend the cash needed to keep going, and which project shareholders pick once they do. Exercise 10 (Amaro de Matos 3.1.5 #9): the formal Myers (1977) underinvestment argument. Lista_R4 exercise 4 (Berk 16.25): a firm whose only asset is vacant land and whose debt exceeds its value, facing a positive-NPV project that equity will not fund.

Remedies. Lista_R7 exercise 2 (Grinblatt–Titman 16.14): why existing creditors can gain when a bankrupt firm issues new debt senior to theirs. Exercise 3 (Amaro de Matos 3.1.5 #7): how convertibles, warrants and call provisions reduce the agency costs of debt. Lista_R4 exercise 2 (Grinblatt–Titman 16.11): why short-term debt eases these conflicts.

Renegotiation and bankruptcy design. Lista_R7 exercise 14 (Tirole 3.6): a contract that leaves the borrower too small a stake is renegotiated before effort; the condition for debt forgiveness, its reading as a fictitious fixed-investment project, and why, by rational expectations, nothing is gained by planning on renegotiation. Exercise 17 (Tirole 7.3): the firm may later reallocate funds between keeping up resale value and raising the success probability; how the optimal contract deals with this asset substitution. Exercise 18 (Tirole 4.9): the optimal contract continues only when short-term profit is good news, and short-term debt implements it; then a court that hands half the liquidation proceeds to the borrower, and why this "borrower-friendly" rule blocks financing or raises the bankruptcy probability, hurting the borrower and not the lenders.

Name and correct four misconceptions, each with its mechanism: levered firms always take riskier projects (only when debt is risky and shareholders control the choice; covenants, short maturity and reputation restrain them); any new financing cures debt overhang (junior claims leave the wedge in place; senior or renegotiated claims remove it); renegotiation is always welfare-improving (ex post yes, ex ante it softens incentives and invites strategic default); a positive correlation between interest rates and default proves adverse selection (moral hazard predicts it too, which is why Karlan and Zinman randomise).

Close by connecting forward: Topic 5 removes verifiability altogether; with income that cannot be seen, the only stick left is the threat to cut off future funding.
