No arithmetic on air. Argue each claim, say when it fails, and name the later topic where it bites.

One identity, two kinds of promises: firms sell goods and sell promises, senior ones to creditors and a residual one to owners. Argue why assets equal liabilities plus equity in market value, not only in the books: the cash flow from assets is split exhaustively between the two claims, so their values add up. Then stress-test it: this additivity is the engine of Modigliani–Miller in Topic 2A, and it survives any split unless the split changes the cash flows themselves, which is the moral-hazard case of Tirole Ch. 3 later in the course. Use Grinblatt–Titman Ch. 2–3 to contrast the debt and equity instruments behind the two promises.

Book versus market as an information problem: recognition rules that favour tangibles while intangibles may be most of enterprise value; historical cost against market value, and why the Silicon Valley Bank split between held-to-maturity and available-for-sale securities mattered for a bank funded short. Explain how Starbucks reached negative book equity with a market cap above 120 billion dollars, why that is not insolvency, and why market leverage and net debt measure leverage better even though covenants are written on book numbers. Link to Higgins §1.7 and Berk §2.3.

Ratios as diagnostics: the DuPont identity and the slide's question "should managers maximize financial leverage?" Answer it with the three ROE problems in Higgins §2.3 (timing, risk, book value) and preview the formal answer: by MM II the required return on equity rises with leverage, the same logic as the bank-capital debate in Topic 2B. Present ROIC as a financing-neutral measure and say where it still misleads, for example when investment is expensed rather than capitalised. Explain why growth, risk and leverage all move the P/E, and why EBITDA, coverage and economic earnings answer different questions. Cover margins, receivable and payable days, and operating leverage briefly.

The income statement: accrual and matching, depreciation as capex spread over the asset's life, gross profit through EBIT to net income, and why mostly-fixed operating costs create operating leverage, so that sales shocks hit fixed-cost firms hardest. Note that net working capital and the current and quick ratios measure short-term liquidity, and that trade payables are a source of finance (the GPA balance sheet), which leads into the trade-credit topic later in the course.

Profits are not cash: walk through the three sections of the cash-flow statement, and every operating adjustment and why it is signed as it is. Define unlevered free cash flow and free cash flow to equity, which value each one gives, and why interest is excluded from the first: the tax benefit of debt is handled later through the discount rate or the APV (Topic 2B). Discuss PS1 Exercise 1, on net income versus FCF and whether to add back deferred taxes. Then the uses of free cash flow (creditors, reinvestment, cash reserves, dividends, buybacks) as two open questions: does payout policy matter (dividend irrelevance, Topic 2B), and what do managers do with free cash, the agency and liquidity issues of Tirole Ch. 5?
