Explain and discuss at graduate depth Topic 2A of Corporate Finance: valuation, the NPV rule and the Modigliani–Miller frictionless benchmark. Sources: `Aula_Topic2A - Valuation and MM.pdf` (the slides), `Lista_PS1.pdf`, `Lista_PS2.pdf`, `Lista_R2 - Valuation.md`, `Lista_R3 - MM, taxes and trade-off.md`, `Livro_Berk DeMarzo Harford - Fundamentals of Corporate Finance 2e (2011).pdf` (hereafter Berk), `Livro_Grinblatt Titman - Financial Markets and Corporate Strategy 2e (2002).pdf` (hereafter Grinblatt–Titman), `Livro_de Matos, João Amaro - Theoretical Foundations of Corporate Finance (2002, Princeton University Press) [10.1515_9780691188478] - libgen.li.pdf` (hereafter Amaro de Matos, Ch. 1–2) and `Livro_Jean Tirole - The Theory of Corporate Finance-Princeton University Press (2005).pdf` (hereafter Tirole). No arithmetic on air. Derive each result in words, state the exact assumptions it uses, and say what breaks when one fails. Listeners know Topic 1: market-value balance sheets and unlevered free cash flow.

Discount rates: expected versus realised returns, spot rates, and the risk premium as pay for systematic risk only (Berk Ch. 12). Derive the unlevering formula: apply the linearity of covariance to assets equal to debt plus equity to get the asset beta as a value-weighted average. Edge cases: risky debt has a nonzero beta, so use bond indices; averaging the comparables' equity betas is wrong; with taxes the tax shield enters the balance sheet (Topic 2B). Walk through Lista_R2 exercise 6 (Grinblatt–Titman 11.10–11.13) and exercise 9 (a divisional WACC, Berk 13.20).

Cash flows and the NPV rule: incremental, opportunity and sunk costs (PS1 Exercises 2–3; Lista_R2 exercise 11), and why cash flows and rates must match, enterprise with WACC and equity with the cost of equity (Lista_R2 exercise 10). State the conditions under which NPV equals the gain to shareholders: symmetric information, competitive financiers, no risky debt. Then break each one: with risky debt in place, shareholders may reject positive-NPV projects (debt overhang, Topic 2B and Tirole Ch. 3); under asymmetric information, issuing equity to fund a good project can lower the share price (Tirole Ch. 6). IRR pitfalls via Lista_R2 exercises 7 and 8.

Debt versus equity: payoff, seniority, maturity, control rights, covenants and technical default, limited liability. Show from the two payoff lines that equity is a call on the assets, and flag the conflict it creates when risk can be chosen (risk-shifting, Topic 2B).

Complete markets: replication, the law of one price, and why no arbitrage is equivalent to positive state prices (Amaro de Matos §1.2, Grinblatt–Titman Ch. 7). Explain the risk-neutral measure, why the slump claim is costlier, and Fisher separation under uncertainty: with complete markets, investment choices do not depend on preferences (Lista_R2 exercises 14–15). Ask what changes when markets are incomplete and a new security can span a new state.

The penniless entrepreneur as a proof by construction: price the project from the stock and the bond, then show that under debt, equity or any mix the firm is worth the same and the entrepreneur keeps the whole NPV, because every financier breaks even. Note the slides' check: replicating each position directly with traded securities gives the same answer.

MM I, two proofs: the arbitrage proof with homemade leverage, and why it needs investors to borrow on the same terms as firms (Lista_R3 exercise 15, Amaro de Matos 2.1.5 #3–4); then risky debt as a safe bond minus a put, equity as a call, and put-call parity making their sum independent of the face value (Berk §21.5–21.6; PS2 Exercise 1). Lista_R3 exercise 11 (Grinblatt–Titman 14.2–14.3) shows MM surviving risky debt and new debt of equal seniority, which dilutes the old creditors. Add Tirole's state-by-state identity (Ch. 2 §2.2, p. 78), why it needs no risk neutrality, and its one real failure: the claim structure changes non-contractible effort.

MM II: the cost of equity rises linearly in the debt-equity ratio while debt is safe, then flattens as the cost of debt rises, and the WACC stays fixed. Discuss Lista_R3 exercise 5 (Berk 16.5: why maximising the expected return on equity is not the goal) and PS2 Exercise 4 (Yerba: EPS up, P/E down, no gain). Retell the "borrow more, value rises" fallacy from the slides. State the conservation-of-value principle and why the yield overstates the cost of risky debt.

Correct four misconceptions: debt is cheap financing; MM needs risk neutrality; a positive NPV always helps shareholders; equity beta measures project risk.

Close with what Topic 2B relaxes: taxes and default costs.
