The State-Price Balance Sheet

A one-period firm with two equally likely states, priced by two state prices. Raise the face value of debt and watch the split between debt and equity move while the value of the assets stays put (MM I). The cost of equity climbs in a straight line while debt is safe and goes flat once debt can default (MM II).

Ricca, Topic 2A slides 55–81 (entrepreneur example), 90–96 (risky debt, put–call parity), 97–105 (MM II, rD vs. the yield). State probabilities fixed at 1/2 (slide 55).

Xb · boom payoff

What the project's assets pay at t = 1 if the economy booms.

Xs · slump payoff

What the assets pay in the slump. Debt with face at or below the smaller payoff never defaults.

pb · boom state price

Price today of 1 paid only in the boom. Cheap, because an extra unit is worth little when everyone is rich.

ps · slump state price

Price today of 1 paid only in the slump. With pb it fixes the risk-free rate: 1 + rf = 1/(pb + ps).

F · face value of debt

The fixed repayment promised at t = 1. Creditors get min(X, F); shareholders get max(X − F, 0).

Expressions (state probabilities q = 1/2): A = pbXb + psXs (slide 68); D = pbmin(Xb, F) + psmin(Xs, F) and E = pbmax(Xb − F, 0) + psmax(Xs − F, 0) (slides 44, 90–93); 1 + rf = 1/(pb + ps); rk = E[payoffk]/valuek − 1 for k = D, E, A (slide 18); put P(F) = pbmax(F − Xb, 0) + psmax(F − Xs, 0), so D = F/(1 + rf) − P(F) (slide 92); yield y = F/D − 1 (slide 104). Defaults reproduce slides 68–71: A = 1.75, D = 1, E = 0.75, NPV = 0.75, rD = 5%, rE = 40%, rA = 20%. The default state prices are the exact fractions; the sliders display them rounded to 0.357 and 0.595, and dragging either replaces the exact value with the slider grid.
