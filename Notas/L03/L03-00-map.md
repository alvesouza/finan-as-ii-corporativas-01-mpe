# Lecture 3 (Topic 2B) — Taxes, bankruptcy costs and the static trade-off · Map of the notes

> **PDF.** [L03-notes.pdf](L03-notes.pdf) — this whole unit in one printable file (rendered from these notes; rebuild with `python -I ~/.claude/commands/templates/notes_pdf.py Notas/L03` after any edit).

> **One claim.** **Taxes break MM through one door**: interest is deducted, payouts to equity are
> not, so $V_L=V_U+V_{ITS}$ and the gain goes to shareholders. **What $V_{ITS}$ is worth depends on
> the debt policy** — a fixed debt gives $\tau D$, a target ratio gives $\tau r_DD/(r_U-g)$ — and
> that choice is the whole difference between **APV and the after-tax WACC**, which agree exactly
> when $D/E$ is constant. **Taken alone the shield says "all debt"; the deadweight cost of default
> says "not so fast"**, and the static trade-off puts the optimum where
> $\tau[1-H(K^*)]=C\,h(K^*)$. **Every purely financial decision, payout included, stays a zero-NPV
> transaction** once these two frictions are accounted for.

Three layers per section:

| Layer | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **U** — intuition | the two income statements, Higgins's Timid/Bold, the evidence on leverage, the investment-distortion numbers |
| **C** — course (examinable) | everything on slides 2–51 and 54–63: MM I with taxes, the balance-sheet identity and both MM II formulas, the three $V_{ITS}$ and WACC forms, APV vs. WACC, the trade-off model and its FOC and statics, the bank-capital statements, MM dividend irrelevance, the appendix derivation |
| **G** — graduate | Miller (1977) personal taxes, DeAngelo–Masulis (Amaro §2.2). **Named, not examinable** |

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;V_L=V_U+\underbrace{\frac{\tau\,E[\min(X,K)]}{1+r_f}}_{V_{ITS}}-\underbrace{\frac{C\,H(K)}{1+r_f}}_{PV(\text{bankruptcy costs})},\qquad \tau\,[1-H(K^*)]=C\,h(K^*)\;}$$

> With $C=0$ it is MM I with taxes ($V_L=V_U+\tau D$, Amaro Prop. 2.1.3); with $\tau=0$ it is MM
> (safe debt is irrelevant); with both it is the trade-off — **one formula, three lectures' worth of
> results.**

## Scope and ceiling

- **Examinable:** slides 2–51 and the appendix 54–63, at the level of the C sections; the
  trade-off model with a general $h$ (FOC, SOC, signs) and in the uniform case.
- **Named only:** the empirical papers on slides 34–35 and 38–39 (read their findings, not their
  methods); the bankruptcy procedure (Ross §16.10); Miller (1977) and DeAngelo–Masulis (G).
- **Out (scope guard):** dynamic trade-off and refinancing models, pecking order and signalling
  (Topic 6, asymmetric information), the formal agency models behind debt overhang and
  risk-shifting (Topic 4) — bridged to, not written up.
