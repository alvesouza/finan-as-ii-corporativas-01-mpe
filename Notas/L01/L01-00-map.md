# Lecture 1 — Introduction and accounting review · Map of the notes

> **PDF.** [L01-notes.pdf](L01-notes.pdf) — this whole unit in one printable file (rendered from these notes; rebuild with `python -I ~/.claude/commands/templates/notes_pdf.py Notas/L01` after any edit).

> **One claim.** **A firm sells goods and sells promises**, and its balance sheet records the split of
> one stream of cash into senior promises (liabilities) and a junior residual (equity). **Because the
> split is exhaustive, $A=L+E$ holds in market value, not only in the books**, and that additivity is
> the seed of Modigliani–Miller. **The books measure that split at historical cost and on accruals**,
> so book equity can be negative while the market values the firm at 122.6 bn (Starbucks), and profit
> can be positive while cash runs out. **Ratios mix operations with financing**: DuPont shows ROE
> rising on leverage alone, and ROIC removes it, with
> $\mathrm{ROE}=\mathrm{ROIC}+\frac DE[\mathrm{ROIC}-r_D(1-\tau)]$. **Free cash flow** is the number valuation
> will use: the cash the assets release, measured as if all-equity.

## The one result to hold

$$\boxed{\;\mathrm{ROE}=\mathrm{ROIC}+\frac{D}{E}\Big[\mathrm{ROIC}-r_D(1-\tau)\Big],\qquad \mathrm{ROIC}=\frac{\mathrm{EBIT}(1-\tau)}{D+E}\;}$$

> **ROIC is the business; the bracket is the spread; $D/E$ scales it.** Leverage raises ROE only if
> the business earns more than debt costs after tax, and it always multiplies ROE's swings by $1+D/E$.
> Whether that higher, riskier ROE is worth anything is the question the next two lectures answer:
> no in a frictionless market (MM, L02), and a tax-versus-distress trade-off otherwise (L03).
