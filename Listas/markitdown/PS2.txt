Exercise 2 Company A currently has no debt in its capital structure and 10 million shares
outstanding. The beta of its equity is 1.20. Company A’s expected future free cash ﬂow is
$25 million per year in perpetuity, starting next year. Company A is considering changing
its capital structure by issuing debt and using the proceeds to buy back stock in such a
way that it will have a constant debt-equity ratio of 25% after the change (continuously
adjusted). Assume that A’s debt cost of capital will be 3%. The corporate tax rate is
25%, and there are no other market imperfections. Assume that the CAPM holds, the
risk-free rate is 3%, and the expected return on the market is 8%.

(a) Compute the rate of return of the equity before and after the change in the capital
structure.

(b) Compute the ﬁrm’s new WACC.

(c) Compute the value of the all-equity ﬁrm and the stock price before and after the
announcement of the change. Find the value of the levered ﬁrm and the tax shield (after
the change). Find the stock price and the new number of outstanding shares after the
issue.

(d) Assume only for this question that company A issues debt, but instead of keeping a
constant debt-equity ratio equal to 25% the ﬁrm keeps interest expenses equal to 20% of
its free cash ﬂow. Compute the value of the tax shields in this new situation.

(e) Assume only for this question that company A issues debt, but instead of keeping a
constant debt-equity ratio equal to 25% the ﬁrm issues $100 million of perpetual debt.
Compute the value of the ﬁrm’s tax shields, the stock price, and the new number of
outstanding shares after the issue.

Exercise 3 Consider an entrepreneur with a project that requires an investment of
I = 60 today. The project generates (unlevered) free cash ﬂow of 100 or 60 tomorrow
with probability 50% each. The expected return on assets is rA = 15%. Assume there
are no market imperfections and corporate taxes are zero.

(a) Find the value of the all-equity ﬁrm.

(b) Suppose the entrepreneur chooses to raise 60 by issuing debt and that creditors
demand a return of 6.6%. What is the face value of debt? What is the market value of
equity?

(c) Now assume that if bankruptcy occurs, the ﬁrm must pay 7% of the realized cash
ﬂow to lawyers (direct bankruptcy costs). Find the face value of debt and the market
value of equity. Which party is “paying” for the bankruptcy costs?
